# WHO Dietary & Hydration Guidelines for the Elderly

## 1. General Energy Requirements
As individuals age, their basal metabolic rate declines, often accompanied by reduced physical activity. Consequently, energy requirements are lower.
- For elderly men (60+ years), daily energy needs typically range between 1800 to 2200 kcal depending on activity level.
- For elderly women (60+ years), daily energy needs range between 1600 to 1900 kcal.
- It is critical to maintain nutrient density because while energy needs decrease, the requirement for essential vitamins and minerals remains unchanged or even increases.

## 2. Hydration Guidelines
Dehydration is a common and serious condition in older adults due to a decreased sensation of thirst and age-related changes in kidney function.
- A minimum fluid intake of 1.5 to 2.0 liters per day (approximately 6 to 8 glasses of water) is recommended unless contraindicated by medical conditions such as congestive heart failure or advanced kidney disease.
- Warm fluids, herbal teas, soups, and water-rich foods (cucumbers, melons) can help meet hydration targets.

## 3. Fiber and Digestive Health
Constipation and sluggish digestion are prevalent in older adults.
- The dietary guidelines recommend an intake of 25 to 30 grams of fiber daily.
- Good sources include whole grains (oats, brown rice, whole wheat), pulses, fruits (papaya, apples), and vegetables.
- Adequate hydration must accompany high fiber intake to prevent intestinal blockage.

## 4. Bone Health (Calcium and Vitamin D)
Age-related bone loss increases the risk of osteoporosis and fractures, especially in post-menopausal women.
- Calcium requirements for adults over 60 are approximately 1000 to 1200 mg per day.
- Good sources include low-fat milk, yogurt, paneer, ragi (finger millet), sesame seeds, and dark green leafy vegetables.
- Vitamin D is critical for calcium absorption. Regular sunlight exposure and fortified foods or supplements are recommended.
