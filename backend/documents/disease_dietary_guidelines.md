# ICMR Disease-Specific Dietary Guidelines for Elderly Patients

## 1. Diabetes Mellitus (High Blood Sugar)
Management of diabetes in the elderly requires maintaining stable blood glucose levels and avoiding hypoglycemia (which can lead to falls and confusion).
- **Carbohydrates**: Prioritize complex carbohydrates with a low glycemic index (GI). Restrict refined sugars, white bread, white rice, sweets, and honey. Recommend whole grains (oats, barley, brown rice, whole wheat) and millets (ragi, jowar, bajra).
- **Fiber**: High fiber intake (>30g/day) is beneficial for glucose control. Include green leafy vegetables, beans, and seeds.
- **Meal Frequency**: Smaller, frequent meals (e.g., 5-6 meals including mid-morning and evening snacks) help prevent blood sugar fluctuations.
- **Safety Veto**: Never recommend sugary desserts, sweetened beverages, or heavy high-GI fruit juices.

## 2. Hypertension (High Blood Pressure) and Cardiovascular Disease
Dietary interventions are crucial to control blood pressure and reduce risk of cardiovascular events.
- **Sodium Restriction**: Limit daily salt intake to less than 5 grams (about 1 teaspoon). Avoid processed foods, pickles, papads, canned foods, salted snacks (namkeen, chips), and baking soda.
- **Potassium**: High potassium intake helps lower blood pressure. Recommend bananas, oranges, spinach, coconut water (unless kidney disease is present).
- **Fats**: Limit saturated fats (ghee, butter, palm oil) and trans fats. Prioritize monounsaturated (MUFA) and polyunsaturated (PUFA) oils like mustard oil, olive oil, sunflower oil, or rice bran oil. Avoid deep-fried foods.

## 3. Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD)
Kidney function declines naturally with age, and patients with CKD require strict dietary restrictions.
- **Protein**: Restrict protein intake in early-to-moderate CKD (typically 0.6 to 0.8 grams per kg of body weight) to reduce workload on the kidneys. For advanced stages, strict monitoring is required. Avoid excessive protein supplements.
- **Sodium & Fluid**: Fluid restriction is often necessary if there is edema (swelling) or oliguria. Limit sodium strictly.
- **Potassium**: Restrict high-potassium foods (bananas, potatoes, citrus fruits, coconut water) if blood potassium is elevated, as hyperkalemia is dangerous for the heart.
- **Phosphorus**: Restrict foods high in phosphorus, such as dairy products, nuts, seeds, and cola drinks.

## 4. Osteoarthritis and Osteoporosis
- Increase calcium and vitamin D intake (milk, curd, ragi, green vegetables).
- Include anti-inflammatory omega-3 fatty acids (flaxseeds, walnuts, chia seeds, fatty fish like salmon or mackerel).
- Maintain healthy weight to reduce load on knee and hip joints.
